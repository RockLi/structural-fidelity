Input and Horizon Dependent Structural Fidelity in Chunk 
Coupled Spiking Networks
Xinjun Li
NEXT BRAIN LTD
Preprint  •  9 November 2026
Abstract
A fixed structural edit can preserve a spike sequence under one input and change it under another. We  
study  this  dependence  using  statistics-preserving  edge  swaps,  controlled  inputs  and  finite-horizon  
parameter certificates. The original 125-chunk numerical model is tested on ten artificial graphs from three  
connection rules. Despite identical total external drive at every slot, global and focal inputs change the  
visibility of 4–13 of nineteen fixed swaps per graph. A paired homogeneous and heterogeneous leaky  
integrate-and-fire validation in brian2-atlas reuses three originals and extends observation from 30 to 300  
ms under a constant background drive sufficient for isolated-cell firing. Heterogeneous short-window  
switching persists, but by 300 ms every tested swap differs from its reference, saturating the binary  
visibility readout. Fixed-support weight families provide a complementary regional guarantee. Positive  
certified  intervals  cover  4,308  of  4,320  LIF  direction  conditions  and  include  distinct  floating-point  
configurations. A post hoc comparison finds a long-window failure inside the short-window certified  
interval in 710 of 720 heterogeneous conditions; 394 of 829 direction pairs ordered under both matched-
drive inputs change order at 300 ms. Input-specific filtering and threshold-margin scores retain useful  
ordering but degrade with horizon and do not consistently beat activity controls. Earlier temporal-error,  
paired-dose and unsuccessful structural-selection results delimit what these findings imply. Together, the  
evidence links input-conditioned structural effects to finite-horizon fidelity regions in two deterministic  
kernels, without establishing universal topology rankings, model compression or asymptotic robustness.
1 Introduction
Which changes of a network's endpoints matter for its trajectory after node-specific connection statistics  
are preserved? A structural intervention need not change the effective input under every source activity  
pattern. Even when inputs change, thresholded spike outputs can remain equal, and temporal or population  
aggregation can conceal differences that are present in fine trajectories. These distinctions make the input  
protocol and observation rule part of a fidelity claim.
Efficient neural simulation has  long used the  consolidation of  convergent additive  inputs  [1,2], with  
communication  and  accumulation  organized  by  channel  and  delay  [3].  We  use  this  principle  as  an  
execution control in a scheduler where arrivals can activate dormant chunks. Embedded spiking networks  
and population descriptions address the further task of reducing represented dynamics [4–7], while brian2-
atlas describes execution contracts for heterogeneous simulation [8]. Our control retains adjacency and unit 
states, allowing subsequent comparisons to change the graph without conflating them with a different  
input-delivery rule.
Structural effects on neural dynamics are well established. Motif analysis [9], network downscaling [10],  
equitable partitions and cluster synchronization [11,12], degree-preserving rewiring [13], and structure–
activity and structure–correlation studies [14–16] describe complementary aspects of this relationship. Our  
question concerns cross-run trajectories of the same identified chunks under fixed node-specific degree and 
strength summaries. It does not infer a new structural principle from the fact that rewiring changes activity.
The  temporal  resolution  of  a  spike  comparison  is  also  substantive.  Metric-space  and  filtered-spike  
approaches explicitly parameterize sensitivity to timing and cumulative count differences [17,18]. We  
therefore examine both exact slot counts and readouts that retain different amounts of temporal and node-

identity  information.  Triangle  inequalities  guarantee  that  certain  aggregations  cannot  increase  L1  
discrepancy; they do not determine how much of a particular intervention's discrepancy will disappear.
We distinguish endpoint replacement from fixed-support weight perturbation. Statistics-matched rewiring  
and single legal double-edge swaps test which identified spike effects depend on input; reference-activity  
graph selection [19] provides a negative control on inferring closed-loop fidelity from an open-loop score.  
Temporal-error  composition,  paired  doses  and  initial-state  controls  characterize  the  observed  
discrepancies.  The  discovery  input/readout  analyses  are  post  hoc  and  use  seed  99  over  30  ms;  
prospectively fixed graph and LIF comparisons test the input effect beyond that discovery setting.
Sensitivity and finite-time spike preservation have established context. In vivo experiments demonstrate  
propagation of a single extra cortical spike [20]. Distance to threshold sets has been used to characterize  
spiking dynamics [21], prescribed spike trains can be expressed as parameter constraints [22], and learning  
precisely timed spikes links synaptic weights, threshold crossings and reset history [23]. Stability regions  
around trajectories have also been studied in recurrent spiking models [24]. These studies motivate the  
distinction  between  sensitivity  and  preservation;  they  do  not  establish  the  mechanism  of  the  present 
deterministic networks. Our contribution concerns auditable region guarantees under a specified numerical  
execution contract and their input and horizon dependence, rather than the first observation that threshold  
margins or stable regions exist.
Neural-network verification provides related methods for guarantees over sets of perturbations. Symbolic  
interval propagation has been used to bound outputs of ReLU networks [25], while temporal encodings and 
SMT constraints have been used to verify SNN classification robustness under input-spike perturbations  
[26]. Our verification target is preservation of the complete labeled finite spike record in a recurrent  
simulation under fixed-support joint weight perturbations and a specified floating-point execution contract.  
The enclosure principle is established; the present study develops and audits its model-specific realization  
and examines the resulting guarantees across inputs and observation horizons.
The  article  addresses  two  connected  questions:  whether  matched  input  summaries  determine  a  fixed 
structural edit’s spike effect, and which nonzero weight perturbations are guaranteed to preserve a finite  
reference  sequence.  Ten  original  graphs  from  three  connection  rules  test  the  first  question.  Paired  
homogeneous and heterogeneous LIF networks in brian2-atlas reuse three originals and examine both  
questions over nested 30/100/300 ms prefixes. Certified lower bounds and actual replay failures distinguish 
regional preservation from observed equality and locate horizon-dependent losses. Supporting execution,  
readout and selection controls remain part of the evidence; full proofs and the separate LIF enclosure  
derivation appear in the article and its appendices. Edge swaps and fixed-support weight families are  
different interventions; the latter are not a complete mechanism explanation of the former. Results first  
present swap visibility and its graph and LIF validations, then regional guarantees, horizon failures and  
direction comparisons, followed by supporting temporal-readout, dose and selection controls.
2 Models and fidelity conditions
2.1 Chunk graph and unit dynamics
The original execution benchmark is a directed graph of C chunks. Each chunk contains U = 100 allocated  
units, giving N = CU. Chunks have either excitatory or inhibitory source identity, with 80% excitatory  
chunks in every workload. A projection connects a source chunk to a destination chunk and carries a  
nonnegative weight. When source chunk j emits  S j[n] spikes in slot n, explicit routing generates one  
message of magnitude we S j[n] for each outgoing edge e. The destination broadcasts the increment to all  
of its units in the appropriate excitatory or inhibitory input channel. The graph therefore specifies chunk-
to-chunk coupling.

These equations define a simplified numerical spiking model without a biophysical calibration of voltage  
or conductance units. The timestep is dt = 0.1 ms; the code uses its numerical value in the Euler update.  
With uninterrupted updates and no input, voltage leaks toward zero, not the reset value. The kernel has no  
membrane capacitance, reversal potentials or lower voltage bound; its input states enter as current-like  
numerical quantities. The shortest permitted interval between spikes is two slots, or 0.2 ms at this timestep.
Before each active chunk update, local fields contribute 0.01 F E and 0.01 FI to the corresponding unit  
input states. Afterward the fields decay with a 10 ms time constant, and the chunk spike count is added to  
F E . This update also applies to inhibitory chunks; FI starts at zero and has no spike-driven source, so it  
remains identically zero in these workloads. Identical initial conditions, parameters and broadcast inputs  
keep units within a chunk synchronized. N thus describes the allocated workload, while the number of  
distinct unit trajectories is at most C.
2.2 Scheduling and delivery time
All routing variants use the same active, pending and dormant chunk scheduler. Due messages wake their  
destination. A stepped chunk remains active when it spikes, receives an event, or has a local field or input  
state above the scheduler tolerance of 10 . Future queued events also retain scheduler activity. Skipped⁻⁶  
unit states are frozen; voltage alone does not trigger an update. The explicit reference is defined by this  
scheduler together with the numerical kernel.
Routing occurs after the harness advances its current slot counter. For the uniform stored edge delay of one  
used here, a spike emitted in slot n produces explicit delivery in slot n + 2. The raw-sum, static-gain and  
history-modulated paths use the same arrival slot. The two-channel and filtered four-channel paths deliver  
in slot n + 1. These are comparisons between complete execution rules: the filtered and history-modulated  
methods differ in arrival time as well as their input transformations (Figure 1).

The  following  proposition  applies  the  established  superposition  principle  for  additive  inputs  [1,2]  to 
discrete routing and activation. It specifies a sufficient execution condition, including preservation of  
events  that  wake  dormant  chunks.  Linearity  is  required  of  input  accumulation,  not  of  the  complete  
neuronal dynamics.

Proposition  1  (Sufficient  condition  for  grouped  delivery).  Consider  a  deterministic  discrete-time  
simulation with nonnegative message magnitudes and separate excitatory and inhibitory input channels. At  
each routing step, group positive-amplitude messages only when destination, input channel and arrival slot  
agree, replacing each nonempty group by a message carrying its summed amplitude. Assume that each  
delivery only adds the message magnitude to its designated input accumulator, apart from activation  
bookkeeping, and that all messages due in a slot are accumulated before a common neuronal update.  
Assume identical initial states, external inputs, integration rules and routing phases, and scheduling that  
depends on state and message existence and arrival time, rather than message multiplicity, identity or  
ordering. Preserve separately any zero-amplitude events that have activation effects. Then explicit and  
grouped  routing  produce  identical  neural  dynamical  states,  spikes  and  activation  decisions  in  exact  
arithmetic.
Proof. For fixed source spike counts, each group delivers the same input increment by associativity and  
commutativity of addition. Positive amplitudes and separate polarity channels preserve the existence of a  
delivery event. This preserves the set of pending destination–channel–arrival-slot triples after each routing  
step, as well as event existence at delivery. The identical states and fully accumulated inputs therefore give  
the same subsequent integration and activation decisions. The resulting spikes generate the same aggregate  
inputs at the next routing step. Induction over slots completes the argument. Queue representations and  
message counters may differ.
This sufficient condition describes an algebraic property of the execution rule. Equivalence is relative to  
the fixed discrete reference; it does not establish exact integration of a continuous-time model. Floating-
point addition can depend on accumulation order, especially near a firing threshold. The experiments  
therefore measure actual population-count agreement separately; they do not assume that all floating-point  
states are identical.
2.4 Approximation controls
Filtered  and  history-modulated  outputs  are  heuristic  implementation  controls.  They  alter  delivered  
magnitudes  and,  for  the  filtered  variants,  arrival  time.  A  static-gain  ablation  and  raw  sums  remove  
successive  output  transformations.  Appendix  H  specifies  all  equations,  parameters  and  earlier  
comparisons; these variants do not represent the space of local circuit reductions.
2.5 Execution cost
All methods retain the unit-state arrays and projection adjacency. At each slot, the aggregation index scans  
source slots and sums the spike flags of each source with replaced outgoing edges. It traverses those edges  
only when that source has a nonzero spike count. The raw-sum and history-modulated paths also scan all  
target slots to clear accumulators, update internal states and emit messages. With uniform delay, these  
paths emit at most two aggregated messages per target receiving positive aggregate input, one for each  
polarity.  This  bounds  the  output  message  count,  while  source  scans,  edge  traversal  and  neural-state  
integration remain.

2.8 Finite horizon certification with nonzero input differences
The exact-input corollary guarantees a stronger equality than is needed for identical spike outputs. We  
additionally enclose candidate continuous states under the reference branch history, routing the reference  
source spikes through the candidate graph in actual source order. Each affected target must preserve  
execution or skipping, threshold decisions, reset and refractory updates, status labels and next-active  
membership, including pending arrivals. Local fields can be reused only after the common spike and  
execution history has been established. The sufficient threshold checks are:
V hi  <  θ   ( reference  silent ),      V lo  ≥  θ   ( reference  spike )    (9)
Here V = [ V lo , V hi] encloses the candidate pre-reset voltage and θ = 30. The first condition applies to a  
silent reference update and the second to a reference spike, only at refractory-eligible executed updates.  
Failed or unresolved checks are not predictions of spike divergence. Soundness is a joint induction over  
slots and affected targets, not an assumption that one changed target may be treated as correct without  
checking it. Appendix J supplies the statement, proof and numerical enclosure contract. The guarantee  
permits unequal continuous states and ends with the declared window.
The singleton checker uses conservative binary32 rounding bounds [27]. The optimized implementation  
computes outward binary64 bounds and falls back to exact rational arithmetic when a projected binary32  
endpoint is unresolved. The family checker retains affine dependence on fixed parameters plus a uniform  
residual enclosure; exact support bounds and a rigorous corner-envelope implementation are compared on  
the same boxes. Ordinary endpoint or grid replay is a different baseline: it checks specified points and does  
not certify the unsampled interior. For a single specified candidate, however, a complete local consistency  
replay can itself establish the common-branch property by the same induction. We therefore do not claim a  
logical advantage over local replay for singleton verification.

3.2 Measurements and analysis scope
The population activity in slot n is y[n] = S[n]/(N dt s), where S[n] is the total emitted spike count and dt s 
is the timestep in seconds. Temporal agreement is measured by zero-lag Pearson correlation r between the  
reference and approximate per-slot series, without smoothing, trial averaging or lag optimization. Mean-
rate error is δ FR = 100(mean( yapprox)/mean( yref) − 1). We also count slots in which the two total population 
spike counts differ. This count tests equality of the measured series more directly than a correlation  
rounded to one.
Message cost is Q = M approx/M ref , where M counts recurrent messages enqueued after simulation steps. It  
excludes externally injected drive and includes messages scheduled beyond the observation window. In  
hybrid runs, M includes both individually routed and aggregated messages. Q measures queue insertions; it  
does not measure runtime or peak memory. It depends on the generated spike activity as well as the routing 
rule, so comparing Q across different activity trajectories does not isolate routing efficiency. Retained-edge 
fraction describes the graph partition and is distinct from the fraction of queued messages using explicit  
routing.
The gain study evaluates a 3 × 3 grid and a second 3 × 3 neighborhood, with one overlapping pair: 18  
evaluations and 17 distinct gain pairs per method. We use r ≥ 0.70 and | δ FR| ≤ 20% as descriptive  
engineering screening criteria. Each execution-benchmark graph setting has one deterministic realization,  
and gain pairs are parameter settings rather than statistical replicates. The raw-sum and static-gain controls  
are post-hoc exploratory analyses; there is no held-out graph set or preregistered inference. Integer totals,  
mismatch counts, numerical summaries and reproducible source are archived.
For the historical workloads, deterministic means a fixed graph and input protocol without additional  
process  noise.  Explicit  routing  uses  an  unspecified  HashMap  traversal  order,  so  floating-point  
accumulation order and bitwise results are not guaranteed to match across runs.

neuron’s membrane time constant. It does not clamp its projected voltage at reference resets or during  
refractoriness, although evaluation uses the reference-eligible mask. Full frozen-branch derivatives retain  
those  operations  and  remain  an  algebraic  comparator.  All  compared  methods  use  the  same  bracket-
resolved pairs. A target pair with no resolved comparison contributes no macro score; coverage and the  
number of contributing targets accompany every principal score. No accuracy is assigned to unresolved  
pairs.

5 Discussion
The controlled-input counterexamples and direction brackets answer different parts of the same question.  
Matched  connection  and  external-input  summaries  do  not  determine  the  effect  of  every  structural  
intervention. Conditional on a specified reference, a sound enclosure can nevertheless identify nonzero  
perturbation families that preserve the finite spike sequence. Input transfer changes some certified direction 
orders, and longer observation can turn a previously safe perturbation into a witnessed failure. These  
findings are complementary, but fixed-support weight certification does not fully explain the effects of  
endpoint swaps.
The original readout and dose results remain important for interpreting this distinction. Within-node time  
accumulation can cancel  much  of  a  local edit’s  fine-slot discrepancy,  and pooled statistics  can hide  
changes at identified nodes. The paired random chains show that increasing edits from roughly 1% to 10%  
adds relatively little to normalized fine-slot L1 while increasing cumulative-count discrepancy. Because  
chains are nonmonotonic, the single-swap-to-smallest-dose gap is unsampled, and L1 near one is not a  
calibrated decorrelation measure, these findings support readout-dependent interpretation rather than a  
critical edit threshold. The failed selection gate likewise constrains the inference from a low open-loop  
input score to closed-loop fidelity.
The LIF comparison removes complete within-chunk synchrony and the legacy unbounded numerical  
voltage scale as necessary conditions for the short-window phenomenon. It also changes several aspects of  
the kernel; only the homogeneous/heterogeneous LIF pair isolates the specified parameter heterogeneity.  
Three reused graphs and one fixed LIF configuration do not establish generality over neuronal models,  
arbitrary heterogeneous connectivity, noise, time discretization or sustained nonperiodic input. The 300 ms  
prefix is ten times the original observation window, not an asymptotic result. The ten legacy graphs and  
three LIF graph selections are reported separately because the latter are a subset.
The LIF background is sufficient for isolated-cell tonic firing. Accumulated phase shifts in this operating  
regime are a possible contributor to long-window equality-label saturation and shrinking certified ranges,  

39
but the present comparisons do not isolate that mechanism or establish that background drive dominates  
the network firing rate. They also do not explain the earlier temporal-error results in the different legacy  
kernel. The evidence does not cover a fluctuation-driven LIF regime with subthreshold background, and  
finite-horizon certification does not imply asymptotic stability.
The score is a reference-dependent diagnostic whose filter and margin reflect the same dynamics used by  
the certificate. Factor comparisons quantify its restricted ordering performance, but neither prove a unique  
causal mechanism nor establish a runtime advantage over direct certification. Likewise, unsuccessful open-
loop graph selection and successful ordering of fixed-support weight directions are different tasks; one  
does not causally explain the other. Approximation and dose results remain informative controls (Section  
4.13 and Appendix H), not evidence that memory proxies compress graphs or accelerate simulation. The  
score-floor audit motivates a future unit-consistent choice proportional to each unit’s threshold-to-reset  
distance. Such a relative floor would require a new fixed protocol and validation; it is not used here, and  
neither improved ordering nor elimination of clipping is established by this post hoc proposal.
The certificate contribution is coverage of an explicitly defined parameter family under an auditable  
execution contract. Numerical validation is necessary for that implementation but does not replace the  
enclosure proof; its many checked state values are not many independent scientific discoveries. Zero lower  
bounds, proof gaps, capped radii, unresolved orders and failed selection gates remain separate outcomes.  
These  distinctions  make  a  finite  fidelity  claim  reviewable  without  assigning  every  failure  the  same  
dynamical interpretation.
The empirical unit of replication must follow the design. There are ten original legacy graphs, with one  
LIF original per connection rule drawn from that same set. Module and ring generators share a weighted  
sampler and are not matched in per-node degree; their comparison is not a causal test of geometry.  
Directions,  inputs,  graph  versions  and  nested  windows  are  dependent.  The  LIF  validation  uses  one  
parameter regime, one timestep, deterministic broadcast projections and a repeated 30 ms drive. Three  
hundred milliseconds extends the original window tenfold but supplies no stationary or infinite-horizon  
conclusion. The 710/720 and 394/829 bracket comparisons are post hoc witnesses within these fixed  
conditions, not population rates or universal laws.
6 Conclusion
In the tested graphs, matching total external drive does not determine a fixed swap’s finite spike effect.  
This  counterexample  survives  a  paired  heterogeneous  LIF  implementation.  Fixed-support  weight  
certificates and replay failures then locate input- and horizon-dependent fidelity ranges: direction order can  
change with input, and longer prefixes  can invalidate perturbations certified for shorter ones. Exact-
equality switching and heuristic score agreement both have observable limits. The resulting contribution is  
a controlled, reproducible account of conditional structural fidelity, with explicit sufficient guarantees and  
retained negative results.

Appendix J Finite horizon certificates and parameter families
J.1 Sufficient condition and proof
Proposition 4 (common branch finite horizon enclosure). Fix a deterministic discrete implementation,  
initial state, external input and finite window. Let a family of candidate graphs change incoming streams  
only at targets A. Construct each candidate stream from the reference emissions, including actual products,  
delivery order, event presence and pending-event effects. Suppose initial candidate states are enclosed,  
interval updates enclose every allowed parameter realization, and all execution, eligible-threshold, status  
and next-active guards resolve to the reference decisions at every affected target and slot. Then every  
family  member  has  the  reference  spikes,  execution  decisions,  status  labels  and  refractory  deadlines  
throughout the window; its continuous states lie inside the enclosures. Incoming streams and initial states  
of  unaffected  targets  must  be  unchanged.  Support-independent  assertions  about  parameter  families  
additionally require support and event-presence invariance or validated set-valued bookkeeping.

59
Proof. At initialization, the candidate states belong to their sets and the initial queues and statuses have  
their  specified values. Assume  jointly  that  all earlier  emissions  and  relevant  branch  decisions  agree.  
Routing these emissions through any fixed candidate gives exactly the conditional event streams used by  
the checker, in their real order, with the true due and pending flags. The scheduler guard therefore fixes the  
same execute/skip decision. Sound event and state updates enclose the true candidate pre-reset voltage.  
The one-sided threshold checks establish the same spike decision, hence the same reset and refractory  
deadline. Local fields depend only on their earlier values, common spikes and execution history, so their  
archived reference values remain valid. The residual-dynamics and pending-event guards establish the  
same status and next-active membership. At unaffected targets the incoming ordered streams and previous  
states agree, so their updates agree. This completes the induction simultaneously over all targets. A first  
disagreement cannot occur while the hypotheses hold. For a parameter set the reasoning applies to every  
member because every update and guard is enclosed uniformly. Failure of a sufficient guard leaves the  
property undecided unless a concrete counterexample is supplied.
The argument does not certify arbitrary candidate trajectories after the first branch mismatch. It permits  
nonzero input and state differences but no unaccounted execution differences. A common spike raster  
alone is insufficient to justify reading reference local fields in this scheduler. Positive retained weights in  
the tested families make due and pending flags parameter-independent under common emissions. The  
proof is an application of enclosure and branch preservation; it does not claim a new general stability  
theorem.
J.2 Arithmetic and affine representation
The  frozen  execution  uses  nearest-even  binary32  operations,  finite  values,  gradual  underflow  and  
unreassociated source order. Let u =  2−24 and η =  2−149
. Each basic operation with exact result z is  
enclosed using error u|z| + η, assuming no overflow [27]. For m sequential additions with m u < 1, the  
checker uses γ = m u/(1 − m u), bounding accumulation error by γ times the sum of operand magnitudes  
plus mη/(1 − m u). The singleton implementation validates outward binary32 projection with exact rational  
comparisons; the optimized path encloses computations in binary64 and falls back to that oracle when  
necessary. The decay factor is the actual rounded binary32(1 − h), h = binary32(0.1).
For voltage, the exact affine expression (1 − h)V + h(E − I) retains contraction, but its error budget follows  
the original operation order: E − I, addition of −V, multiplication by h, then addition to V. If the successive  
rounding bounds are r1 through r4 , the added voltage error is h( r1 + r2) + r3 + r4 . Refractory steps still  
accumulate inputs but hold voltage and conductance decay; skipped states freeze. A common spike resets  
voltage and its uncertainty to −65 while retaining conductance uncertainty. The deposited THEORY.txt  
specifies the interval formulas and every scheduling guard.
For families, each scalar state is represented as c + Σ p j α j + ε with | α j| ≤ 1 and |ε| ≤ r. The scalar box  
enclosure is c ± (Σ| p j| + r). The rigorous corner alternative enumerates the same affine extrema and adds  
the same uniform residual radius, including uncertainty in calculated coefficients. It is therefore a same-
guarantee baseline; ordinary corner simulation is not. Entry weights are rounded once to binary32 and  
emitted events are rounded again after multiplication by 100. Both errors, sequential addition and the  
original voltage/decay rounding are enclosed. The retained coefficients are validated quantities, not exact  
unvalidated  derivatives.  Full  formulas,  assumptions  and  exact-Fraction  oracle  code  are  included  in  
THEORY-AND-METHODS.txt and oracle.py.

M.3 Sufficient branch proof and failure semantics
Proof. The initial states satisfy the enclosure premise. Assume enclosure containment and equal discrete  
histories up to an update. Sound propagation contains every candidate next state; guard inclusion forces the 
reference decision for all of them. The same reset and refractory transition then preserves the induction  
premise. Equal emitted spikes preserve subsequent source event streams, while fixed support and ordering  
preserve their routing structure. Unaffected neurons receive unchanged events; all affected neurons are  
enclosed. Induction over every state update and event delivery proves the finite-prefix claim. In the legacy  
scheduler, wake-up, freezing and field guards must also be enclosed. In the clock-driven LIF kernel, the  
same spike history determines refractory eligibility, and all four units in each affected chunk are checked.  
The guarantee is relative to this discrete execution, not to exact continuous-time integration.
This is an implementation-specific instance of Proposition4. All bounds in M.2 must be propagated before  
checking the reference threshold decision. A reset sets voltage slope and remainder to zero, while synaptic  
enclosures  persist. Equal spike history fixes the refractory mask; voltage cannot be treated as freely  
evolving during the clamp, and currents cannot be frozen with it. An unresolved guard rejects the sufficient 
certificate. Only an actual replay discrepancy is an unsafe-point witness. Thus interval conservatism,  
iteration limits, zero radius resolution and genuine failure remain distinct outcomes.
